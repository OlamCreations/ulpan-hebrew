Tehilim 51: Create for me a pure heart

תְּהִלִּים נ״א · לַמְנַצֵּחַ מִזְמוֹר
HomePs 51
The confession psalm, headed by the one moment in David's life he would least want quoted, and then read for three thousand years by everyone else. Verse 17, the request that God open the speaker's lips, is said silently at the start of every Amidah, so most of this psalm's users meet it one line at a time without knowing where the line comes from. For a learner it is the best drill in the book for the imperative with an object suffix: wash me, purify me, create for me, restore to me, uphold me, deliver me. Count how many verbs end in the same two letters and the grammar teaches itself.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

Two verses of heading, then four movements. Verses 3 to 11 ask for the record to be erased and the man to be washed, in the vocabulary of laundry and ritual purity. Verses 12 to 14 stop asking for cleaning and ask for manufacture: בְּרָא (bra, v.12), create, and then renew and restore. Verses 15 to 19 turn outward, promise a lesson, and redefine what an offering is. Verses 20 and 21 leave the individual entirely for Zion and its walls. Those last two verses ask for a rebuilt city and working sacrifices right after the psalm has said that sacrifices are not what is wanted; many readers take them as a later addition made when the psalm entered communal use, and others read the whole thing as one composition that moves deliberately from the person to the nation. The question is disputed and the text gives no way to settle it.

רמז · Remez: allusion

The cleansing words are borrowed from the sanctuary. בְאֵזוֹב (ve-e-zov, v.9), hyssop, is the plant used in the Torah to sprinkle the blood on the doorposts in Egypt, to purify a person healed of the skin affliction, and in the water of purification made with the red heifer. So the speaker is asking to be treated as a contaminated object handled by a priest, not as a defendant. Against that, בְּרָא (bra, v.12) is a different register altogether: it is the verb of Genesis 1, and in biblical Hebrew only God is ever its subject, so what is asked for in verse 12 is not repair but a new thing. הֶרֶב (he-rev, v.4) is an imperative meaning make much, used adverbially, which is why English versions say thoroughly. מִדָּמִים (mi-da-mim, v.16) is literally bloods, plural, and it can mean bloodguilt or a violent death; which one David is asking to be spared is not decided by the grammar.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

The tradition reads verse 15 as the answer to an obvious question: why is this in the book at all, and why with the heading naming Nathan and Bath-sheba. The reading is that the fall is recorded so that the repentance can be, and that a man who had not fallen could not have taught this. So the psalm is treated as the working manual for teshuvah rather than as one king's private business, which is why it belongs to the penitential material rather than to the historical books. Two of its lines were then lifted out and given jobs of their own: שְׂפָתַי (sfa-tai, v.17), my lips, opens every Amidah, and verse 12 is sung on its own to more tunes than any other verse here. Read that way, the psalm answers Nathan's accusation not by defending anything but by making the accusation public property.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

The mystical reading takes verse 19 literally as a theory of offerings: the altar receives what is broken, and a whole heart has nothing to put on it. Breaking is therefore not the damage that has to be repaired before service can start, it is the material of the service. לֵב (lev, v.19), heart, is described as both broken and crushed, two words for the same state, and the reading turns the crushing of verse 10 into the currency of verse 19. Alongside this runs the reading of verse 12 as creation from nothing performed inside a person: the old heart is not cleaned, it is replaced, and the request is possible only because the verb belongs to the one who made the world out of no prior thing. The two readings meet at the same place, which is that nothing usable survives the process except what is given back.

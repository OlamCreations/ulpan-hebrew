Tehilim 12: Save, LORD; the faithful are gone

תְּהִלִּים י״ב · לַמְנַצֵּחַ עַל
HomePs 12
Nine verses about speech, and almost every line is somebody talking. Smooth lips, a doubled heart, a boastful tongue, then God's own sentence quoted in the middle at verse 6, then a claim about what divine words are made of. It falls on the second day of the monthly cycle, where the reading runs from Psalm 10 to Psalm 17. The heading at הַשְּׁמִינִית (hash-mi-nit, v.1) names something musical that nobody can now define, and the same word heads Psalm 6.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

The architecture is simple once you notice the psalm is about mouths. Verses 2 to 5 are human speech, first described and then quoted. Verse 6 is divine speech, quoted. Verse 7 is a statement about the material divine speech is made of. Verses 8 and 9 are what follows for people who have to live among the first kind. The turn in the middle is not a rescue, it is God speaking, and the psalm treats that as the answer to the problem of everyone else speaking.

רמז · Remez: allusion

Three cruxes, all in the second half, and none of them settled. At יָפִיחַ (ya-fi-ach, v.6) the verb means to blow or to pant, and the clause has been read as the one who is panted at, the one who pants for safety, and the one who is treated with scorn; the JPS line above chose the puffing reading. At בַּעֲלִיל (ba-a-lil, v.7) the noun is rare enough that it is read either as a crucible or as an adverb meaning plainly, and the JPS line chose crucible. At תִּצְּרֶנּוּ (titz-re-nu, v.8) the vocalized suffix is third person singular, him, although the verb just before it is them, and the JPS line above renders it us. The printed translations do not agree with one another here.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

The seven of שִׁבְעָתָיִם (shiv-a-ta-yim, v.7) does what seven usually does in the Bible, standing for completeness rather than counting to a number, and the image was read as a claim about kind rather than degree: divine words are not truer on average than human ones, they have had the alloy taken out of them entirely. The doubled heart of verse 3 reads as a plain description of a divided self, and the correction it implies is not to speak less but to have one heart, so that whatever gets said matches what is behind it. Read that way the psalm is not a complaint about liars, it is a complaint about a whole society in which nothing said can be relied on.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

In the kabbalistic frame speech is the medium of creation, so a psalm about corrupted speech is a psalm about a corrupted world, and the furnace of verse 7 is the standard image for a process that removes without destroying. The seven of the last word of that verse lines up with the seven lower measures through which, in that reading, anything spoken above has to pass before it arrives here. Each passage costs something and each one purifies, which is why the number reads as a count of stages rather than as an intensifier.

Tehilim 13: How long, O LORD?

תְּהִלִּים י״ג · לַמְנַצֵּחַ מִזְמוֹר
HomePs 13
Six verses, and the shortest distance in the whole book from despair to song. The question at עַד (ad, v.2) and אָנָה (a-na, v.2) is asked four times across two verses and then never again: verse 4 turns it into a request, and verse 6 answers in a tense for something that has not happened yet. It falls on the second day of the monthly cycle, where the reading runs from Psalm 10 to Psalm 17. The verb the enemies get at יָגִילוּ (ya-gi-lu, v.5) is the verb the heart gets at יָגֵל (ya-gel, v.6), and that single root is the psalm in miniature.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

A lament with every part visible and none of them padded. Verses 2 and 3 are the complaint, four times how long. Verse 4 is the petition, three imperatives with nothing between them. Verse 5 is the motive clause, the reason God is given for acting, stated as what the enemy would otherwise get to say. Verse 6 is the trust, and it arrives without anything having changed in the meantime. Nothing in the text reports a rescue. The turn happens inside the speaker, which is why the psalm can be this short.

רמז · Remez: allusion

The idiom at הָאִירָה (ha-i-ra, v.4), give light to my eyes, is ordinary Hebrew for strength coming back into a body; the same expression is used in 1 Samuel 14 when Jonathan eats honey and his eyes brighten. Against it stands the sleep of the same verse, where death is named as the thing slept rather than a state slept through. The root that closes the psalm is worth tracking on its own: at יָגִילוּ (ya-gi-lu, v.5) the foes would rejoice, and at יָגֵל (ya-gel, v.6) the heart will rejoice, one root doing both jobs, so the last verse takes the enemy's own verb away from him.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

The fourfold how long reads as a descent rather than a repetition: forgotten, then hidden from, then left to one's own counsel, and last overtaken by someone else, so the questions travel from God's memory down to the enemy's advantage. The verb at גָמַל (ga-mal, v.6) belongs to the vocabulary of a benefit given freely rather than a debt settled, and it stands in the perfect, so the psalm signs off thanking God for something it never says has happened. Read that way it is a training exercise: it does not record a rescue, it rehearses the sentence a person will need on the other side of one.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

The mystical reading takes the hiding of the face as a technical term rather than a mood. Concealment is a stage in a process, not a punishment, and the four questions of verses 2 and 3 measure how far down the concealment reaches. What ends it here is not the face returning but light entering the eyes, which is the smaller and nearer of the two gifts: the seeker is given enough to see by, not the thing seen. The song of verse 6 therefore belongs to the dark rather than to the dawn, and that is exactly why it can be sung before anything has changed.

Tehilim 66: Come and see the works of God

תְּהִלִּים ס״ו · לַמְנַצֵּחַ שִׁיר
HomePs 66
Twenty verses that open by summoning the whole earth and close with one person paying a private debt. Verses 1 to 12 speak as a we: a nation tested, refined like silver, brought through fire and water. From verse 13 the voice is singular and walks into the house with animals in hand. Nothing marks that seam except the number of the verbs and the suffixes, so the grammar is the argument. The line in verse 12, we came through fire and through water, is still ordinary Hebrew for having survived something.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

Two psalms joined at verse 13. The first half is public and plural: the earth is told to shout, the crossing of the sea is offered as evidence, and the we of the nation reports being tested, netted, ridden over, and brought out. The second half is private and singular: one person walks into the house with animals and pays what he promised while he was frightened. Nothing in the Hebrew marks the seam except the number of the verbs, which is why אָבוֹא (a-vo, v.13) is the word to slow down on. The order matters. The nation is delivered first, then one member of it settles the account.

רמז · Remez: allusion

Three words carry real ambiguity. בַמְּצוּדָה (vam-tzu-da, v.11) is either a stronghold or a hunting net, since the noun is attested in both senses, and translations split on this verse; the surrounding language of weight and constraint favours the net, but the reading is disputed. וְרוֹמַם (ve-ro-mam, v.17) can be read as a verb, He was extolled, or as a noun, high praise, sitting under the tongue like something held in the mouth, and old versions differ. יְכַחֲשׁוּ (ye-cha-cha-shu, v.3) literally means they lie or they deny, and is usually rendered cringe or feign obedience, which is the psalm saying that submission under overwhelming force is not the same thing as loyalty.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

The image in צְרַפְתָּנוּ (tze-raf-ta-nu, v.10), refined as silver is refined, is the psalm's own reading of its history: the trouble in verses 11 and 12 is neither punishment nor accident but process, and the proof offered is that it ended in saturation rather than in loss. Read that way, the first half is a set of instructions for how to speak about a disaster once it is over. אָוֶן (a-ven, v.18) then cuts the other way in the second half: the speaker admits that a prayer can fail, and puts the fault inside the one praying rather than in the one listening. The blessing at the end is therefore not automatic. It says only that this time nothing was turned away, which carries weight because the previous verse allowed that it could have been.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

Read as an inner map, the two halves are two stages of one person. The first is the self as a crowd: many voices, public history, events that happen to a group and can be told as a story. The second is the self as a single speaker who owes something and goes to pay it alone, with the mouth as the instrument. Fire and water in בָאֵשׁ (va-esh, v.12) are the standard pair for the two ways a soul comes undone, by burning and by drowning, and coming out to saturation rather than to dryness is how the psalm says the trial gave more than it took. The refining of silver in צְרַפְתָּנוּ (tze-raf-ta-nu, v.10) makes the same claim from the other side: the metal is not replaced, only the dross leaves.

Tehilim 33: Sing to Him a new song

תְּהִלִּים ל״ג · רַנְּנוּ צַדִּיקִים
HomePs 33
Twenty-two verses, one for each letter of the alphabet, though the psalm is not an acrostic, and one of the few psalms in the first book of Tehilim with no heading at all: it opens on a plural imperative and never names an author. Said on Shabbat and festival mornings among the psalms of praise that come before the Shema. The thing to watch is verses 10 and 11: the same two nouns, counsel and plans, are used first of the nations and then of God, and the second use cancels the first.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

The psalm is built in blocks. Verses 1 to 3 are the call, all imperatives and instruments. Verses 4 and 5 give the reason in a single sentence about word and work. Verses 6 to 9 are creation by speech, closing on the two clauses of verse 9. Verses 10 to 12 apply that speech to history, where what the nations decide gets cancelled. Verses 13 to 15 turn the same God into an observer. Verses 16 to 19 dismiss military power and put an eye where the army was, and verses 20 to 22 answer in the first person plural. The spine is a single verb: וַיַּעֲמֹד (va-ya-a-mod, v.9), what stood at creation, and תַּעֲמֹד (ta-a-mod, v.11), what stands in counsel, are the same root.

רמז · Remez: allusion

Verses 10 and 11 use the same two nouns in the same order. עֲצַת (a-tzat, v.10), the counsel of the nations, is broken; עֲצַת (a-tzat, v.11), the counsel of the LORD, stands. The plans of the peoples and the plans of His heart follow the same pattern. Nothing in the vocabulary marks the second pair as superior, only the verbs do. In verse 7 the word כַּנֵּד (ka-ned, v.7), a heap, is the noun used of the sea standing up at the crossing in Exodus 15:8. The Septuagint read the consonants as a different word and translated a wineskin instead, so in Greek God pours the sea into a container rather than piling it up. Both readings are ancient and the choice between them is still argued.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

Verse 15 is the proof text in Mishnah Rosh Hashanah 1:2 for the image of the new year as a review: all who enter the world pass before Him like bnei maron, a phrase the Talmud then has to explain, and the verse is quoted to say that the one who forms their hearts together considers all their deeds. The whole reading hangs on יַחַד (ya-chad, v.15): it can mean that He forms all hearts at once, or that He forms them alike, or that He takes them in together in one act, and the liturgy depends on the last sense. Verse 6 does separate work. Avot 5:1 counts ten utterances of creation, but Genesis 1 has only nine explicit sayings, and the Talmud supplies the tenth from this verse, reading the first word of the Torah as an utterance in its own right.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

The kabbalistic reading takes verse 6 apart into two channels. The word makes the heavens, the breath of the mouth makes their host, so speech and breath become two grades of one act, the first giving structure and the second giving animation. The ten utterances of Avot are identified in this literature with the ten sefirot, which turns בִּדְבַר (bid-var, v.6) from a report about a past moment into a description of continuous emanation: heaven is not made once, it is being spoken. The three appearances of kindness in the psalm, חֶסֶד (che-sed, v.5) filling the world, לְחַסְדּוֹ (le-chas-do, v.18) reaching those who wait, and חַסְדְּךָ (chas-de-cha, v.22) in the closing request, are read as one flow measured at three points, so the last verse asks only that what already fills the earth be received here.

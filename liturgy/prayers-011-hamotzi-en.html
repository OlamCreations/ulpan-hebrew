HaMotzi: Who Brings Forth Bread

הַמּוֹצִיא · just before eating bread · the anchor of every meal
HomeHaMotzi
The bread blessing, said before eating bread at any meal. After Netilat Yadayim (the hand-washing), say HaMotzi immediately, take a bite of bread, then start the meal. No talking between the wash and the bread. If a meal has bread, HaMotzi covers all the other foods in it: you don't need separate blessings for the salad, the soup, the wine, etc., the bread blessing "includes" the rest of the meal. After the meal, conclude with Birkat HaMazon (grace after meals). Bread is the anchor.

♦ Lesson

  • When, just before eating bread at any meal. Especially Friday night and Shabbat day after Kiddush, but also any weekday meal with bread.
  • After washing, say HaMotzi immediately following Netilat Yadayim. No talking, no checking the phone, no asking someone to pass the salt between the wash and the first bite.
  • What counts as bread, bread made from one of the five grains (wheat, barley, spelt, rye, oats). Pita, challah, baguette, bagel, matzah at Pesach. Not rice bread, not gluten-free almond-flour bread (those get different blessings).
  • The grammar, haMotzi is the hiphil participle of י.צ.א (to go out). Qal would be "goes out"; hiphil = "causes to go out / brings forth". Same root as yetziat Mitzrayim (the Exodus from Egypt), God brought out the bread, just as He brought out the people.
  • Why "from the earth" and not "from the oven", the rabbis (Talmud, Berachot 38a) ask exactly this. Answer: the blessing acknowledges the ultimate source of bread (the earth, agriculture, rain, sun) rather than the proximate human work (baking). The miracle of bread is in the wheat field, not the kitchen.

♦ About this prayer

Twelve Hebrew words. The most-said blessing in observant Jewish life, at every meal with bread, three times a day for many families. The structural opening you'll memorize after a week of practice.

The text: Talmud Berachot 38a

The wording "hamotzi lechem min ha-aretz" is debated in the Talmud. Rabbi Nechemiah preferred motzi ("brings forth"); the rabbis preferred hamotzi (with the definite article). The halacha follows the rabbis. The phrase echoes Psalm 104:14, "motzi lechem min ha-aretz", which is itself a paraphrase of the agricultural cycle described in the Psalm.

Why bread is the master blessing

In Talmudic-era life, bread defined a meal. Other foods could be eaten as snacks, but if bread was set on the table, the gathering was a formal meal. The rabbis encoded this in halacha: bread requires Netilat Yadayim, HaMotzi, and a full Birkat HaMazon after, none of which are required for other foods. The bread blessing also "subsumes" the other foods in the meal: you don't bless the soup separately, since the soup is part of the bread-meal.

This is why Shabbat tables have two challot, they're the formal anchor of the meal, and the double bread also recalls the double portion of manna that fell on Fridays in the wilderness (so the people wouldn't have to gather on Shabbat).

The customs around it

1. Some sprinkle salt on the bread before HaMotzi (or after), echoing the Temple offerings, which always required salt. The custom also says salt averts misfortune, "the salt of the covenant" (Lev 2:13).

2. After HaMotzi, take a piece for yourself first, then pass to others. Don't pass the bread before tasting it.

3. The bread should be whole (a complete loaf, two on Shabbat) when blessed, then cut after. Some communities cut a small notch in the bread before saying the blessing so the cut motion is already started.

Shabbat HaYom: Today is the Sabbath of the LORD

Shabbat day, at the second meal · שַׁבָּת הַיּוֹם
HomeShabbat HaYom
The most cheerful of the lunch zemirot, and the shortest to learn: the refrain is three words, it opens and closes every stanza, and it is a direct quotation of Exodus 16 — Moses telling the people in the wilderness that today is a Sabbath, so do not go out looking for manna. The song takes that instruction and turns it into a menu. Half of it is about what to eat, which for a poem quoting the wilderness is a decent joke.

שָׁלשׁ (sha-losh) and סְעוּדוֹת (se-u-dot) name the three meals of the day, which is the practice this whole set of zemirot is sung across. The poem ends with three imperatives and the word בָנַי (va-nai), my children — the only place the poet speaks in his own voice.

♫ Singing notes

  • The three-word line is the whole song for a beginner. It opens and closes every stanza, so you sing it ten times and everyone else fills in between.
  • Major key, and it should sound like it. C F G, brisk, and the mood is not solemn.
  • The lines are very short — one to four words — so the melody moves quickly. It suits clapping better than the minor-key zemirot.
  • Around 112 BPM. This is the one to start with if the table has been quiet.

♦ About this song

The refrain is a quotation

Exodus 16:25, Moses on the morning the manna did not fall: eat it today, for today is a Sabbath to the LORD, you will not find it in the field. The poem lifts the last clause and repeats it ten times.

Isaiah 58 underneath

Call the Sabbath a delight, and then you shall take delight in the LORD. That verse supplies the third stanza almost entirely, and it is the source of the word for delight that runs through the whole Sabbath liturgy.

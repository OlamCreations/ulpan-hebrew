Tehilim 25: To You, LORD, I lift up my soul

תְּהִלִּים כ״ה · לְדָוִד אֵלֶיךָ
HomePs 25
An alphabetical acrostic: one letter of the alphabet opens each verse, in order, alef through tav. The order is not quite clean. No verse opens with vav and none with qof, resh opens two verses in a row, and a twenty-second verse beginning with pe stands outside the sequence altogether. Twenty-two verses of asking, with a block of teaching in the middle: verses 1 to 7 are petition, verses 8 to 15 turn to the third person and describe the kind of God this is, and then the petition resumes and closes on the whole nation rather than one man. In the Sephardic rite it is the psalm of nefilat apayim, said with the head lowered during weekday Tachanun. Watch two roots recur: the root for waiting and the root for being put to shame.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

Three movements. Verses 1 to 7 ask, and what they ask for first is instruction, not rescue: make known, teach, lead. Verses 8 to 15 stop addressing God and describe Him instead, in the third person, and every verb the speaker had requested comes back as something God simply does. Then verses 16 to 21 return to the first person and to the concrete situation, which turns out to be loneliness, guilt, a net, and enemies. Verse 22 stands apart: no I, no me, only Israel. The teaching block in the middle is not a digression. It is the answer the speaker gives himself before asking again.

רמז · Remez: allusion

The acrostic is the frame and its faults are the interesting part. One letter opens each verse in sequence, though in the first two verses the letter sits on the second word rather than the first. No verse begins with vav, and none with qof: רְאֵה (re-e, v.18) takes the resh slot, and רְאֵה (re-e, v.19) repeats resh where qof should have stood. Then פְּדֵה (pe-de, v.22) adds a twenty-third line beginning with pe after tav has already closed the alphabet. Readers have long noticed that the three verses standing outside the strict run, the alef of אֵלֶיךָ (e-lei-cha, v.1), the lamed of לְמַעַן (le-ma-an, v.11), and the pe of פְּדֵה (pe-de, v.22), spell the name of the first letter, which is also the root of a verb meaning to teach. Psalm 34 shows the same missing vav and the same extra pe verse. Whether that is design or the residue of transmission is argued and not settled. Two roots also carry across the psalm: waiting, at קֹוֶיךָ (ko-vei-cha, v.3), קִוִּיתִי (ki-vi-ti, v.5) and קִוִּיתִיךָ (ki-vi-ti-cha, v.21), and being put to shame, at אֵבוֹשָׁה (e-vo-sha, v.2) and אֵבוֹשׁ (e-vosh, v.20). Note also רֵיקָם (rei-kam, v.3), an adverb that can mean either without cause or empty-handed, and the ambiguity changes who is being described.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

Verse 8 is read as the ground of repentance itself: the object of the instruction is sinners, not the righteous, and the verse gives God's own goodness as the reason. On that reading the door is open before anyone knocks. Verse 11 is read the same way, as a claim made on the name rather than on merit, since a debt that is called great is not thereby easier to cancel. The request of תִּזְכֹּר (tiz-kor, v.7), not to remember the sins of youth, has been read not as a plea for erasure but as a plea about timing: not that they be undone, but that they not be brought forward now. And the psalm's use in Tachanun rests on פְּדֵה (pe-de, v.22), which turns twenty-one verses of one man's trouble into a communal prayer without changing a word of what came before.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

The word at סוֹד (sod, v.14) is the one later Jewish mysticism takes as its own name, the fourth level of reading. In this verse it does not yet mean a secret doctrine. It means the close circle where a decision is discussed before it is announced, and the psalm says that circle is open to those who fear Him. The second half of the verse is the point: the covenant is there to make it known to them, so the secret is a thing transmitted rather than withheld. Read this way the psalm's opening line is already the whole method. Lifting the soul, at אֶשָּׂא (e-sa, v.1), is the raising of what is lowest toward what is highest, and everything asked for afterward, the ways, the paths, the truth, is the route by which the lifted thing is meant to come back down again with something in its hands.

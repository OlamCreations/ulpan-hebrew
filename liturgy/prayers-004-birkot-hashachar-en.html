Birkot HaShachar: Morning Blessings

בִּרְכוֹת הַשַּׁחַר · opens Shacharit · ~15 micro-blessings, one per morning act
HomeBirkot HaShachar
The morning blessings, a chain of ~15 short blessings recited at the start of Shacharit (the morning service), each thanking God for some daily act of restoration. Each blessing corresponds to a moment in the waking sequence: hearing the rooster crow (waking up), opening eyes (sight), standing up (no longer bound), getting dressed (clothing), beginning to walk (firm steps). The series turns the ordinary biological acts of waking into a chain of conscious gratitude, by the time you finish, you have explicitly noted ~15 things that could have gone wrong but did not. Talmudic origin (Berachot 60b), with the exact list standardized by the Geonim (~8th-10th c.). This page teaches 5 representative blessings, the full series adds blessings for being Israel, being free, being male/female, etc.

♦ Lesson

  • When, the opening section of Shacharit (the morning service), right after Modeh Ani and the washing of hands. Each blessing was originally said at the moment of the corresponding act (hearing the rooster, opening eyes, standing up), but today they are all said together at the start of the formal service.
  • The order matches waking:
    1. Hearing the rooster (first awareness of dawn).
    2. Opening eyes (sight returning).
    3. Standing up from bed (no longer bound by sleep).
    4. Getting dressed.
    5. Walking firm steps.
    6. Putting on a belt (girding the body).
    7. Putting on a head covering (kippah/hat).
    8. Walking to synagogue (steps of the upright).
    9. Various identity blessings, being a Jew, being free, being male/female.
  • Vocabulary, participles of doing: notice that each blessing uses a present-tense participle (one who does X) rather than a past-tense verb. Pokeach (opening), malbish (clothing), matir (releasing), noten (giving), all qal or hiphil active participles. God is continuously doing these things, not did them once and stopped.
  • The identity blessings (controversial):
    • She'asani Yisrael, "who made me an Israelite/Jew" (Sephardi version)
    • She'lo asani goy, "who did not make me a non-Jew" (older Ashkenazi version)
    • She'lo asani aved, "who did not make me a slave"
    • She'lo asani ishah (men), "who did not make me a woman", or in Conservative/Reform/some modern Orthodox versions, replaced by she'asani b'tzelem Elohim ("who made me in the image of God").
    • She'asani kirtzono (women), "who made me according to His will".

    These have generated significant theological debate in the modern period; many contemporary siddurim use revised wording. The older formulae predate modern egalitarian sensibilities.

  • The rooster's understanding, the very first blessing is about the sechvi (rooster). The Talmud (Berachot 60b) explains the word sechvi with reference to Job 38:36: "who gave the rooster the discernment to distinguish day from night." The rooster crows before dawn, it knows the day is coming before humans do. The first morning blessing thanks God for the intelligence of even the rooster.

♦ About this prayer

A chain of 15 micro-blessings that turns the mundane mechanics of waking into a sequence of explicit gratitude. The bridge between Modeh Ani and the formal morning service.

Talmudic origin: Berachot 60b

The Talmud lists ten or so blessings, each tied to a specific physical moment of the morning. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi (~3rd c. CE) reportedly said each blessing in real time, actually waiting until he heard the rooster before saying the rooster blessing, actually opening his eyes before saying the sight blessing, etc. The custom of saying them all together at synagogue (rather than at home, throughout the morning) developed in the Geonic period. Today most observant Jews say them at synagogue at the start of Shacharit, but some say them at home as part of the early-morning routine.

The Psalms imagery

Several of the blessings quote or paraphrase Psalm 146:7-8:

פֹּקֵחַ עִוְרִים, יְהוָה זֹקֵף כְּפוּפִים, יְהוָה מַתִּיר אֲסוּרִים
"The LORD opens the eyes of the blind, the LORD raises up those who are bowed down, the LORD frees the bound."

Each line of the Psalm became a blessing in Birkot HaShachar. By saying them in sequence each morning, the worshiper enacts the Psalm: I was bound by sleep, God released me. I was lying flat, God stood me up. I was blind in darkness, God gave me back my sight. Each morning is a small redemption.

Why a sequence of 15 short blessings (vs. one long one)

The rabbis could have combined the gratitudes into a single blessing: "Blessed are You, who gives sight, clothing, strength, freedom, etc." Instead they separated them. Each gift is a separate occasion for attention. The cumulative effect is different from a summary, by the end of Birkot HaShachar, the worshiper has paused 15 times. Each pause is an explicit act of recognition. The day starts with one's attention having been trained, blessing by blessing, on what could so easily go wrong but did not.

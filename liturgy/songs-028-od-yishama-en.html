Od Yishama: Yet again shall be heard

Jeremiah 33:10-11 · כֹּה אָמַר
HomeOd Yishama
The single most-sung wedding verse in Hebrew, and the reason it works is the sentence it is lifted out of. Jeremiah is describing a ruin: a place with no people and no animals, empty streets, a city everyone has written off. Then the same sentence keeps going and says that in that exact place there will be heard the voice of joy, the voice of gladness, the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride. The wedding song is the back half of a sentence about a wasteland, and singing it without knowing that loses most of it.

The wedding song is the first stich alone: five occurrences of קוֹל (kol) and its paired form וְקוֹל (ve-kol), four of them naming a sound that was absent from the ruin. The pairing of חָתָן (cha-tan) and כַּלָּה (ka-la) is the line everyone knows, and it is the fourth item in a list, not the subject of the verse. What is being promised is noise in an empty street.

♫ Singing notes

  • The sung portion is the first stich of the second stanza, repeated until the dancing stops. Everything else on this page is context.
  • It is a dance tune. Once the circle is moving, the tempo goes where the circle takes it and the guitar follows.
  • Two phrases, then two phrases: שָׂשׂוֹן (sa-son) and שִׂמְחָה (sim-cha) answer each other, then חָתָן (cha-tan) and כַּלָּה (ka-la) do. Sing it as four short arcs, not one long line.
  • There are many melodies for this verse and they are all in current use. Ask which one before you start playing, or start singing and let the room correct you.

♦ About this song

The text

Jeremiah 33:10-11. Jeremiah 33 is written from a siege — the prophet is under guard and the city is about to fall — and the chapter is a series of promises made at the worst possible moment. This is one of them.

The quotation inside the quotation

From הוֹדוּ (ho-du) to חַסְדּוֹ (chas-do) the verse quotes the Temple thanksgiving formula, which appears word for word in several psalms. Jeremiah is not composing that line; he is predicting that people will be heard saying it again. The wedding song therefore contains a psalm inside a prophecy inside a wedding.

Why weddings

The bridegroom and the bride appear in this verse as a measure of whether a place is alive. A wedding is the moment the community can point at the verse and say it has come true, which is why it is sung at that moment and not, for instance, at a memorial.

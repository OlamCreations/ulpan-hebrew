Tehilim 23: The LORD is my shepherd

תְּהִלִּים כ״ג · מִזְמוֹר לְדָוִד
HomePs 23
Six verses, the best known in the whole book, and the shortest route into biblical Hebrew: almost every word is a noun with a possessive suffix or a verb with an object suffix, which is the single grammatical pattern the psalms live on. Said at the third Shabbat meal, at funerals, and at the table before kiddush in many homes. The imagery turns halfway through, from a shepherd in the field to a host at a table, and the pronoun turns with it.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

A shepherd's day, then a host's evening. Verses 1-4 are pasture, water, path and valley; verses 5-6 are a table, oil and a house. The hinge is אַתָּה (a-ta, v.4): up to that point God is spoken about, from there God is spoken to, and the change of person is the psalm's real argument. Note that the valley is walked through, not avoided, and that the enemies of verse 5 are still present at the meal. Nothing is removed; something is added.

רמז · Remez: allusion

The word rendered "deepest shadow", צַלְמָוֶת (tzal-ma-vet, v.4), is read by the Masoretes as a compound of shadow and death, and older translations give "the valley of the shadow of death". Modern philology usually reads it as a single noun meaning deep darkness. Both readings survive in synagogue use, which is why the verse is said at a funeral and also by a traveller at night. The rod and the staff of שִׁבְטְךָ (shiv-te-cha, v.4) are a shepherd's two tools: one to strike off predators, one to lean on and to steer with.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

The rabbis noticed that יִרְדְּפוּנִי (yir-de-fu-ni, v.6) uses the verb of pursuit, the same verb an enemy would take, and read it as a deliberate inversion: what chased David through the book of Samuel is replaced here by goodness and covenant kindness doing the chasing. Midrash Tehillim ties the table of verse 5 to the wilderness generation, fed in the open while surrounded, and reads the psalm as Israel's biography compressed into six verses rather than one man's.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

In the kabbalistic reading the two halves map onto two modes of divine relation: the shepherd who leads from outside and above, and the host who is present at the same table. The move from third person to second is then not a literary effect but a description of drawing near, and the overflowing cup of verse 5 is the standard image for a measure that receives more than it can hold and passes the surplus on.

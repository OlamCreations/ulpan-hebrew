Tehilim 72: Give the king Your judgments

תְּהִלִּים ע״ב · לִשְׁלֹמֹה אֱלֹהִים
HomePs 72
A coronation prayer, and the best place in the book to learn one verb form: almost every line is a third person jussive, the wish form, so the psalm reads as a chain of "may he" rather than a report of what a king did. Only two psalms carry Solomon's name in the heading, this one and 127. It closes Book II of the Psalter, which is why verses 18 and 19 sound like a benediction rather than a continuation of the argument, and why verse 20 is a note from an editor rather than a line of poetry. Watch where the psalm puts its one word for because: the whole map of tribute in verses 8 to 11 hangs on the reason given in verse 12.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

A prayer at a coronation, in three movements. Verses 1 to 7 ask for justice and describe it as weather and harvest. Verses 8 to 11 widen to the whole map, sea to sea, every king, every nation. Verse 12 then gives the reason with a single word, for, and the psalm spends verses 12 to 14 on one subject only: the needy who cries out, the poor with no helper, the life bought back from fraud and violence. Read in that order, the empire of verses 8 to 11 is not the point but the payment. Verses 18 to 20 belong to the book rather than to the poem: two verses of doxology closing Book II, then the colophon at כׇּלּוּ (ko-lu, v.20) noting that David's prayers end here.

רמז · Remez: allusion

The psalm is built out of rare words, and three of them are cruxes. יִנּוֹן (yi-non, v.17) occurs nowhere else in this form; it is usually connected either to a root meaning to sprout or to the noun for offspring, and translations range from endure to take root to propagate. פִסַּת (fi-sat, v.16) is a single occurrence too, guessed from context as an abundance or a spread of grain. צִיִּים (tzi-yim, v.9) is read either as the nomads who camp in the wilderness or as the wild creatures that live there, the same word that appears among the desert animals in Isaiah 13:21, and the two readings pull the verse in different directions. Two further notes. At יִירָאוּךָ (yi-ra-u-cha, v.5) the Masoretic text has they shall fear You, while the Greek reads he shall endure with the sun, which implies a different Hebrew verb; the difference is one of consonants, not of sense, and it is disputed which is original. And מִיָּם (mi-yam, v.8) begins a formula, from sea to sea and from the River to the ends of the earth, that appears word for word in Zechariah 9:10.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

The verb at יִנּוֹן (yi-non, v.17) produced a name. In the Talmud at Sanhedrin 98b the schools of the sages each derive a name for the Messiah from a verse, and the school of Rabbi Yannai derives Yinnon from this psalm. That single reading did a great deal of work: it licensed reading the whole psalm forward, so that the king who receives judgment in verse 1 is not only Solomon but the king still awaited, and the tribute of verses 10 and 11 is not a report of the tenth century but a description of the end. Note what the psalm itself never does: it never names the king, and it never says he is righteous. It asks that righteousness be given to him, which leaves the office and the man carefully separated.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

In the kabbalistic reading a king is a receiver before he is a ruler. Verse 1 sets the pattern, since judgment and righteousness both arrive with the suffix Yours, and everything the king then does in the psalm is a passing on of what he was given. The rain of verse 6 is the standard image for that flow: it does not belong to the field, it does not stop at the field, and it is measured out onto grass already cut. Read this way the two divine names of בָּרוּךְ (ba-ruch, v.18) and the widening of glory to fill the whole earth in verse 19 are not an appendix but the same movement finishing, from a single throne outward until nothing is outside it.

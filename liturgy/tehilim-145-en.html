Tehilim 145: I will exalt you, my God the King

תְּהִלִּים קמ״ה · תְּהִלָּה לְדָוִד
HomePs 145
Twenty-one verses, one for each letter the psalm actually uses. It runs alef to tav in order and skips one letter: no verse begins with nun, so the mem verse at 13 is followed straight away by the samekh verse at 14. Said three times a day in the liturgy, where it is called Ashrei after the two verses fixed to its front and closed with a verse borrowed from Psalm 115. There is no enemy addressed here, no complaint and no request. The only people in it who come to harm appear in the last four words of verse 20.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

An alphabet used as an argument. Each verse takes the next letter and adds one item to a single claim: this King is great, and his greatness is exercised as care. The first half is about speech, who does it and to whom, and the hinge is verse 12, which finally states that the point of all the telling is that people should know. From verse 14 the psalm stops describing praise and starts describing what is being praised: the fallen held up, everything alive fed, nearness to whoever calls. The sequence is not complete. Twenty-one verses carry twenty-one letters, alef through tav in order, and no verse begins with nun; the mem verse at מַלְכוּתְךָ (mal-chut-cha, v.13) is followed immediately by the samekh verse at סוֹמֵךְ (so-mech, v.14).

רמז · Remez: allusion

The gap falls between מַלְכוּתְךָ (mal-chut-cha, v.13) and סוֹמֵךְ (so-mech, v.14). A copy of this psalm found at Qumran carries a line in exactly that position beginning with the missing letter, and the Greek and Syriac versions carry a matching line, usually rendered as God is faithful in his words and gracious in all his deeds. Whether that line fell out of the text the Masoretes preserved or was supplied later by someone completing the alphabet is disputed, and the manuscripts on their own do not settle it. One detail worth weighing either way: the supplied line closes with the same three words that close the tsadi verse here, וְחָסִיד (ve-cha-sid, v.17) to מַעֲשָׂיו (ma-a-sav, v.17). On a smaller scale, אֶרֶךְ (e-rech, v.8) and אַפַּיִם (a-pa-yim, v.8) read literally as long of nostrils, the Hebrew idiom for a slow temper, which is why the phrase cannot be put into English without losing the face it is describing.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

The Talmud in tractate Berakhot asks straight out why this psalm has no nun verse, and answers that the letter would have started the word for falling, which is not something an alphabet of praise was going to carry. The same passage then points at the verse that does follow, סוֹמֵךְ (so-mech, v.14), which opens with the samekh of upholding: the fall is left unwritten and the holding up is written in its place. That passage is also where the practice of saying this psalm three times a day is grounded, and it singles out one verse as the reason, פּוֹתֵחַ (po-te-ach, v.16) to יָדֶךָ (ya-de-cha, v.16), you open your hand. The codes take that seriously enough to rule that a person who says the psalm without attending to that verse says it again.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

Read as a ladder, the psalm climbs from one mouth to all flesh, and the rungs are the letters. Kabbalistic reading treats the alphabet as the set of instruments the world was made with, so an alphabetical psalm is the whole toolkit turned back toward its owner. That makes the absent letter the point rather than a defect: the one thing the instruments are not used to spell is the fall, and the psalm passes it in silence and goes straight to the verse about holding people up. The open hand is read in the same key, as the standard image for an outflow that is not measured against the worth of what receives it, which is why the verse can say every living thing and leave it unqualified.

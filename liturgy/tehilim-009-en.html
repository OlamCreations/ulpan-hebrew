Tehilim 9: I will praise You with all my heart

תְּהִלִּים ט · אוֹדֶה יְהֹוָה
HomePs 9
A psalm of thanksgiving to the LORD as righteous judge of the nations. David sings of enemies who stumble and vanish (v.4), of the throne set up for judgment that abides forever (v.8), of the LORD as misgav, a high stronghold for the oppressed (v.10). It is a partial alphabetic acrostic that does not close here: the letters continue into Psalm 10, so that Ps 9-10 form a single broken alphabet, praise interrupted by the cry against the wicked who go unpunished.

שִׁיתָה (shitah), "Set / put." Morah is read either as "fear / awe" (from ירא) or "a teacher / razor", the Masoretes again marked it uncertain. The final lesson the nations must know (yed'u): enosh hemmah, "they are but mortals." The psalm ends, like v.17, on סֶלָה (selah), the held pause.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

A thanksgiving for victory and a plea for ongoing justice. V.1: the superscription, al muth labben, whose meaning is already lost to the Masoretes ("upon the death of the son"?). V.2-3: "I will praise You, LORD, with all my heart… I will tell all Your wonders." V.4-7: the enemies retreat, stumble, and perish; the LORD has blotted out their name forever; the cities are torn down, their memory lost. V.8-9: but the LORD abides forever, His throne set up for judgment; He judges the world with righteousness. V.10-11: He is a stronghold for the oppressed; those who know His name trust Him. V.12-21: a summons to sing, a remembrance of the blood of the afflicted (v.13), a prayer from the gates of death (v.14), the nations sunk in their own pit (v.16), and the closing plea that the needy not be forgotten forever (v.19).

רמז · Remez: allusion

The key word is מִשְׂגָּב (misgav, v.10), repeated twice in one verse, a high place, a rock fortress out of reach, from the root שׂגב, "to be high, inaccessible." God is not a wall around the oppressed but a height they are lifted into. Note the mirrored verb in v.16: the nations sink (טָבְעוּ) in the pit they made (עָשׂוּ), caught in the net they hid (טָמָנוּ), three verbs of their own labor turned against them. And v.13, dorésh damim, the One who "requires" / "seeks out" blood does not forget the cry of the anavim (the afflicted); the same root דרשׁ returns in v.11 as those who seek Him (dorshecha): God seeks the blood, and He is sought by the faithful.

דרש · Drash: interpretation

This psalm is an unfinished alphabet. Letters א through כ run across Psalms 9 and 10 (ט falls here at v.16, tavu), but the sequence stutters and breaks. The Sages read this brokenness as deliberate: praise cannot be a closed, perfect form while the wicked still prosper and "the needy is forgotten" (v.19). The acrostic is a vessel cracked on purpose, so that the cry of Psalm 10 can pour through. The throne konen lammishpat (v.8, "established for judgment") answers the toppled thrones of the nations (v.7): one seat falls, one seat stands forever.

סוד · Sod: mystical meaning

Two thrones face each other across this psalm. The thrones of the nations are torn down (v.7), their very names blotted out (v.6, machita), erasure from the Book. Against them, the throne of the LORD is konen (v.8), firmly set, like the world established at creation. מִשְׂגָּב (misgav) names the dimension of ascent: the oppressed dakh (the crushed, the thin one, v.10) is raised from sha'arei mavet, the gates of death (v.14), to the sha'arei bat-Tziyon, the gates of the daughter of Zion (v.15), a journey from the lowest gate to the highest. And הִגָּיוֹן סֶלָה (higgayon selah, v.17) marks where words must stop: higgayon, a low murmur or string-tone, then selah, the held silence. The judgment is known not by speech but by the pause that follows it.

Tehilim 119: Happy are those whose way is blameless

תְּהִלִּים קי״ט · אַשְׁרֵי תְמִימֵי
HomePs 119
The longest chapter in the Bible, 176 verses on one subject, and an alphabetical acrostic built to be recited in pieces rather than read straight through. That is how the widespread custom works of saying the stanzas that spell a person's name at a grave or on a yahrzeit. For a learner it is the best vocabulary drill in the book: eight nouns and a short list of imperatives come back verse after verse, so the fiftieth verse costs a fraction of the first. Start with the nun stanza and its best known line, דְבָרֶךָ (dva-re-cha, v.105), a lamp for my feet.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

Twenty two stanzas of eight verses. Inside a stanza every verse begins with the same letter: eight verses on alef, then eight on bet, through to eight on tav. Checked against this text the pattern holds in all twenty two stanzas without a single exception, so it is the skeleton of the poem rather than an ornament on it. There is no narrative. Nobody is rescued, no enemy is named, nothing is settled between the first verse and the last, and the psalm ends with the speaker still lost: תָּעִיתִי (ta-i-ti, v.176), I have strayed. What moves is not a plot but a position, restated 176 times under pressure. The unit to read is the block of eight, not the single verse.

רמז · Remez: allusion

Eight nouns carry the whole psalm: instruction תוֹרָתֶךָ (to-ra-te-cha, v.34), testimonies עֵדְוֺתֶיךָ (ed-o-tei-cha, v.14), precepts פִּקּוּדֶיךָ (pi-ku-dei-cha, v.27), statutes חֻקֶּיךָ (chu-kei-cha, v.5), commandments מִצְוֺתֶיךָ (mitz-o-tei-cha, v.6), judgments מִשְׁפָּטֶיךָ (mish-pa-tei-cha, v.20), word דְּבָרֶךָ (dva-re-cha, v.16), saying אִמְרָתֶךָ (im-ra-te-cha, v.11). It is often said that one of the eight appears in every verse. Counted against this text the claim is close but not exact: 172 of the 176 verses carry at least one, 166 carry exactly one, six carry two, and four carry none. Verses 3 and 37 fill the slot with a ninth word, way, בִּדְרָכָיו (bid-ra-chav, v.3) and בִּדְרָכֶךָ (bid-ra-che-cha, v.37). Verses 90 and 122 have nothing of the kind. Whether the eight are real synonyms is argued. The roots pull apart, statutes from engraving, precepts from appointing, judgments from judging, testimonies from witness, while the psalm uses them interchangeably often enough that many readers treat them as one word with eight faces.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

Read it as a list of requests and the picture sharpens. The most repeated is not for knowledge but for life: חַיֵּנִי (cha-ye-ni, v.25), keep me alive, nine times. Teach me, לַמְּדֵנִי (lam-de-ni, v.12), eight times. Give me understanding, six. Open my eyes, גַּל (gal, v.18), once, and that one is the sharpest, because it assumes the text is already in front of him and locates the failure in the seeing. Taken together the petitions read less like praise of the law from someone who has mastered it and more like the working prayer of someone studying it badly and in trouble. The custom of reciting the stanzas that spell a mourner's name, then the four letters of neshamah, uses the alphabet the same way: something you walk through, not something you admire.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

The letters are the material, not the label. In the kabbalistic reading the twenty two letters are what the world is made of, so spending all of them, eight verses each, on a single subject is a statement about scope rather than a memory aid: whatever the letters can build, this is what it is for. Eight is the number just past a finished seven, the day after the full week, and it is the size of every stanza here. The tav stanza then closes not on arrival but on תָּעִיתִי (ta-i-ti, v.176), straying, and asks to be searched for. The alphabet is complete and the speaker is not, which is the point.

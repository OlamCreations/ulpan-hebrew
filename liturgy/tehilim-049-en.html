Tehilim 49: No one can pay the ransom for a life

תְּהִלִּים מ״ט · לַמְנַצֵּחַ לִבְנֵי
HomePs 49
A wisdom poem sitting inside the Psalter, closer in vocabulary to Proverbs and Job than to the prayers around it. It is not addressed to Israel and it does not ask God for anything: it summons all peoples, rich and poor together, and argues one case, that money buys nothing at the only transaction that matters. In many communities it is the psalm said in a house of mourning, replaced by Psalm 16 on days when the penitential prayers are omitted. For a learner it is a workout in the third person plural possessive: their wealth, their houses, their dwellings, their soul, their form, ending each time in the same syllable.

♦ PARDES analysis: פרדס

The four levels of Jewish reading of the sacred text: פשט Peshat (literal meaning), רמז Remez (symbolic allusion), דרש Drash (midrashic interpretation), סוד Sod (mystical meaning). The word פרדס means "orchard", each level is a fruit.

פשט · Peshat: literal meaning

A teacher gathers an audience of everyone alive and puts one question: why be afraid of rich men. His answer has three steps. First, no amount of money buys an exemption from death, because the price of a life is simply beyond the market. Second, the rich know this, because they watch wise and stupid people die at the same rate, and they build houses and name land after themselves anyway. Third, since nothing goes down into the grave with them, there is nothing there to fear. Read it as an argument, not as consolation: it never says the rich will be punished, only that they will be ordinary.

רמז · Remez: allusion

Three cruxes. The rare word חָלֶד (cha-led, v.2) in the opening address means something like duration or lifespan, so inhabitants of it are people who live in time rather than in a place, which is already the argument. קִרְבָּם (kir-bam, v.12) as vocalized means their inward part, their private thought, but the ancient Greek and Syriac versions reflect a reading of the same consonants in a different order meaning their graves, giving their graves are their houses forever; the two readings are close to opposite and the choice is disputed. And מִזְּבֻל (miz-vul, v.15) sits in a verse that every commentator marks as textually damaged, with the ancient versions diverging from each other, so any translation of that last line is a reconstruction rather than a rendering. The word עֲקֵבַי (a-ke-vai, v.6) literally means my heels, and readings run from those who trail me to the sin that dogs my steps.

דרש · Drash: midrashic reading

The verb in יִקָּחֵנִי (yi-ka-che-ni, v.16), He will take me, is the verb Genesis 5:24 uses of Enoch, who walked with God and was not, for God took him, and the same root carries Elijah upward in 2 Kings 2. On the strength of that echo the verse has long been read as pointing past Sheol rather than merely away from it, which is why this psalm belongs in a house of mourning: it is one of the few places in the Psalter that says outright that God buys back what money cannot. The two near identical closing lines, verse 13 and verse 21, differ by יָלִין (ya-lin, v.13) against יָבִין (ya-vin, v.21), two verbs of similar shape and unrelated sense. Whether that is a deliberate progression from not lasting to not understanding, or one line is a scribal variant of the other, is genuinely unsettled.

סוד · Sod: mystical reading

In the mystical reading the pairing in verse 3, אָדָם (a-dam, v.3) against אִישׁ (ish, v.3), is not social rank but two grades within a person, the earthbound aspect named for the ground and the aspect capable of standing upright, present in every listener at once. The psalm then reads as an address from one level of the soul to another, and the beast comparison stops being about rich men and starts being about what any person is when the understanding named in the last verse is asleep. Wealth in this reading is a garment: real, worn in the world, and taken off at the door. What God takes in verse 16 is not the man's possessions and not his body but his nefesh, the same word that could not be bought back for any price in verse 9.
